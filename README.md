# Artistic QR Experiences

I reviewed the client brief, the current Artistic QR website, the screenshots you shared, and the reference products. The current site already has the right commercial ingredients—business subscriptions, one-time event services, a logo-based QR test generator, dynamic destinations, and a simple four-step purchase flow—but the experience still feels closer to a premium QR service than a complete self-service SaaS platform.

QRCode AI is useful mainly because it clearly expands the idea of a QR generator into a broader product system: artistic/custom QR creation, dynamic links, branded URLs, analytics, and workspace management. That is the level of product clarity Artistic QR should reach without copying its visual language. HubSpot is useful for tier positioning and feature comparison, while Netflix demonstrates how aggressively a homepage can simplify the value proposition and primary action.

Below is how I would structure the entire Artistic QR product.

1. First: What Artistic QR Actually Is

The most important strategic decision is this:

Artistic QR should not be presented as an “AI QR Code Generator.” It should be presented as a branded QR experience platform.

A normal QR generator solves only this:

URL → QR Code → Scan → URL

Artistic QR should solve this:

Brand / Idea → Artistic QR → Dynamic destination → Digital experience → Management → Analytics

The QR is only the physical gateway.

The real product is everything that happens before and after the scan.

A strong positioning sentence would therefore be:

Artistic QR turns every scan into a branded digital experience you can create, update and measure.

Or a shorter brand-level statement:

More than a QR. A complete experience behind every scan.

3. Dynamic QR Logic

This is one of the strongest things you should communicate in both the website and product.

A dynamic Artistic QR should work approximately like this:

Customer scans QR
        ↓
Artistic QR redirect URL
        ↓
Artistic QR identifies the QR
        ↓
Scan data is recorded
        ↓
Current destination is checked
        ↓
User is redirected
        ↓
Website / Menu / WhatsApp / Smart Page / Video / etc.

Therefore, imagine that a restaurant prints a QR on 5,000 table cards.

Today it points to:

summer-menu.pdf

Next month the restaurant owner changes the destination inside Artistic QR to:

autumn-menu.pdf

The physical QR code does not change.

Nothing needs to be reprinted.

That is a much stronger business benefit than simply saying:

“Create beautiful QR codes.”

The real benefit is:

Design it once. Keep the experience behind it flexible.

7. There Is One Important Business Problem the Client Needs to Decide

This affects both UX and pricing.

Suppose someone pays €10 once for a video QR.

If that QR points through Artistic QR's servers and Artistic QR continues hosting:

 Video

 Dynamic redirect

 Analytics

 Editable content

for the next 10 years, Artistic QR has an ongoing infrastructure cost but receives no recurring revenue.

So a one-time purchase should not accidentally imply unlimited lifetime SaaS infrastructure.

There are three possible models.

Model A — Completely static

Pay once → create → download → destination permanently encoded.

No future dynamic editing.

Very simple.

Model B — Hosted for a defined period

Pay once → QR + hosted experience for, for example, 12 months.

Renew hosting later if required.

Model C — Permanent basic redirect

Pay once → permanent redirect with limited functionality.

Advanced editing, storage or analytics requires subscription.

I recommend a combination of A + B.

Simple link products can be static.

Audio/video/menu/event experiences that require Artistic QR infrastructure should include a clearly defined hosting period.

This needs to be decided before final pricing UX.

Gloop should not simply become a floating chatbot that says:

“How can I help?”

That would add little product value.

Gloop should operate contextually.

On the website

It can answer:

“Which plan should I choose?”

It could ask:

 What will your QR be used for?

 How often will you change the destination?

 Do you need analytics?

 How many QR codes do you need?

Then recommend a product.

During QR creation

Gloop can help with:

 Artistic prompt generation

 Style suggestions

 Color suggestions

 Destination recommendations

 QR naming

 Explaining settings

Example:

“Create an elegant black and gold QR that incorporates my restaurant logo.”

Gloop could turn that into an optimized generation prompt.

Inside analytics

A user could ask:

“Why did my scans increase this weekend?”

Eventually Gloop could summarize activity.

Inside support

It can explain features before escalating to human support.

Therefore Gloop becomes:

Artistic QR's contextual AI product assistant.

It should be available throughout the experience but should not dominate the interface.

9. Recommended Product Direction

There are three possible design approaches.

Direction A — Generator-first

Similar conceptually to QRCode AI.

The homepage immediately puts the generator in front of users.

Advantage: Fast product discovery.

Problem: Artistic QR may feel like another QR generator.

Direction B — Cinematic luxury brand

Heavy Ferrari-inspired motion, large typography, artistic QR imagery and storytelling.

Advantage: Extremely distinctive.

Problem: Users may understand the brand before understanding the software.

Direction C — Product-first premium experience — Recommended

Combine:

Netflix clarity + QRCode AI product clarity + Ferrari-level polish + HubSpot pricing logic.

The product should feel premium, but every cinematic element must demonstrate functionality.

This is the direction I recommend.

The principle should be:

Luxury in presentation. SaaS clarity in interaction.

29. The Biggest Improvement Over the Current Homepage

Your shared redesign already has a strong premium visual direction: dark canvas, gold energy trails, centered AQ branding, artistic QR presentation and high-contrast CTA.

I would keep that visual language.

What I would change is the product storytelling.

At the moment, much of the visual emphasis is:

Artistic QR = beautiful QR generator.

The new homepage should communicate:

Artistic QR = QR design + dynamic destination + digital experience + control + analytics.

That single change will make the redesign feel like a real SaaS product rather than a visually stronger version of the current commercial website.

30. What I Would Not Put on the Homepage

Do not overcrowd it with every feature in the eventual 40-screen product.

I would avoid putting:

 Account management details

 Billing interface screenshots

 Technical API details

 Every QR type

 Detailed security specifications

 Long About Us storytelling

 All event service variations

 Full plan comparison tables

 Gloop as a major standalone section

The homepage's job is not to document the software.

Its job is:

Understand → Believe → Try → Choose → Convert.

31. Product UX Principles I Would Use Everywhere

1. Outcome before terminology

Say:

Where should people go after scanning?

before:

Choose Static or Dynamic QR.

2. Preview everything

Whenever possible, show a phone preview beside the configuration.

3. Never make users fear losing their QR

Show clearly:

QR stays unchanged

when editing a dynamic destination.

4. Make status obvious

Every QR should show:

Draft
Active
Paused
Needs attention

5. One primary action per screen

For example:

Creator → Continue

QR detail → Edit QR

Pricing → Choose plan

6. Explain locked features in context

Don't simply show a lock.

Example:

Advanced analytics are available with Premium.

View Premium

7. Save automatically

The creation workflow should autosave drafts wherever possible.

8. Never hide QR functionality behind visual effects

Animations should communicate product state, hierarchy or cause/effect.

32. My Recommended MVP Priority

For the first product release, prioritize these capabilities above everything else:

Artistic QR generation → dynamic destination → content experiences → QR management → scan validation → download → basic analytics → subscription/billing → support.

Then expand into:

 Brand Kit

 Advanced analytics

 Teams

 Custom domains

 More templates

 Gloop analytics intelligence

 Advanced AI generation

 Integrations/API

This prevents the MVP from becoming a large collection of partially finished features.

33. The Simplest Way to Describe the Entire Product

If you need one sentence to keep your design decisions aligned while working in Figma, use this:

Artistic QR is a SaaS platform where users create visually branded QR codes, control the digital experience behind each scan, update it without reprinting, and measure its performance from one dashboard.

Every important page should support one part of that sentence.

And the central UX concept should always remain:

Create → Connect → Manage → Measure.

That gives you a strong foundation for the homepage, pricing page, public website, onboarding and the complete dashboard without losing consistency as the product expands.
